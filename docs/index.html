Fig. 02 — The Studio · supervise, don't label

The training UI is the fastest way from a video to a deployed detector. The premise: you orchestrate, the machine does the labor.

Busywork never arrives

Frames are sampled at your fps and a perceptual-hash gate drops near-duplicates at ingest — every frame that reaches your screen is worth your attention.

Nudge, don't draw

The trainer runs continuously as you label; each frame arrives pre-filled with the model's current guess. Most are a glance and a Space — and when a guess is off, the drag that fixes it is exactly the training signal the model was missing.

Bad samples get caught

Nothing enters the dataset unconfirmed, and a guardrail watches validation every round — regressions point at the likely-bad samples, clickable, so you fix or drop and move on.

The export is the deployable

The page writes the same int8 ONNX file the Python trainer does, weight for weight — the model you nudged is the model you ship.

Open the Studio →

It trains in your browser, on your GPU (WebGPU: a current Chrome or Edge). Nothing to install, and your frames never leave the machine.

# optional, in the repo: the same page plus a local helper that writes the model to a board

GroundingDINO proposes boxes; a human gate approves them before they train.

03

Train

A tiny soft-argmax CNN, quantized down to an 81 KB int8 ONNX model.
